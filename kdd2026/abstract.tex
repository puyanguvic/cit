\begin{abstract}
Tokenization is a fixed interface between raw symbolic inputs and neural predictors, yet most tokenizers are optimized for natural language.
In structured and semi-structured pipelines, this mismatch can fragment boundary-bearing symbols and high-cardinality values, wasting token budget and inducing irreversible prefix information loss.
We formalize this failure mode as \emph{prefix semantic collapse} and introduce \emph{directed semantic distortion}, a prefix-aligned measure of interface-induced excess risk.
Based on this formulation, we propose \textbf{Controlled Interface Tokenization (CIT)}, which enforces a versioned contract with role-explicit boundaries and typed-value integrity and induces vocabularies by maximizing deployment-aligned compression gain under deterministic, prefix-emitting execution.
On CSIC 2010 HTTP, CIT reduces average length from 330 to 216 tokens and improves drift accuracy from $\approx$0.50 to 0.92 under token-fair budgets.
On Adult, CIT shortens sequences by 43\% and reduces P95 inference latency while matching accuracy.
\end{abstract}
